## 2. Part A — proposal data and obligations

Entered directly in elan.

- **Project title** — concise, no special characters.
- **Summary** — ≤3,000 characters, required in **both German and English**, regardless of
  the language of the project description. Published in GEPRIS if funded, so it must be
  comprehensible to a general audience and must characterise the project as a whole rather
  than only the current funding period.
- **Obligations** — submitting commits the applicant to the principles of good research
  practice and makes the Rules of Procedure for Dealing with Scientific Misconduct
  (VerfOwF) legally binding. Sanctions range from written reprimand to exclusion from
  applying for one to eight years, revocation of funding and repayment.

Write the summary **last**, when the project is settled, and draft the English version
first if the proposal is in English — then translate, rather than composing twice.

## 3. Part B — project description

**Page budget: 25 total — 17 for sections 1–3, 8 from section 4 onward.**
Template formatting must be retained: Arial ≥11 pt body, ≥9 pt publication list, line
spacing ≥1.2.

### 1 Starting point — state of the art and preliminary work

For **new proposals**: explain briefly and precisely the state of the art *in direct
relationship to the project*. Make clear where the applicant's own research sits and where
the unique, innovative contribution will be. Indicate the current state of preliminary work.

For **renewal proposals**: report on previous work. The report must be understandable
without additional literature, and covers the whole funding period even if earlier phases
were reported before.

Requirements that catch people out:

- **Self-contained.** Reviewers are not required to read cited works. Any claim the argument
  depends on must be intelligible from the text.
- **Unlimited citations** permitted in sections 1 and 2, but everything cited must appear in
  section 3.
- **Own preliminary work must be dated**, and if it arose from DFG funding, the relevant
  funding period stage must be referenced in the text.
- **Own vs. others' work** must be distinguishable throughout.

Structural pattern that works: broad field → the specific problem → the gap → why it matters
→ why this applicant can close it. Avoid opening with "In recent years…".

### 2 Objectives and work programme

**2.1 Anticipated total duration.** Intended duration and how long DFG funds are needed. For
ongoing projects, since when the project has been active.

**2.2 Objectives.** Concise description of the research programme and scientific objectives.
Indicate any results relevant beyond science (policy, technology, economy, society). State
objectives as deliverables; every element of the work programme should map to one.

**2.3 Work programme incl. proposed research methods.** The most heavily scrutinised
section. Give a detailed account of the planned steps; for experimental projects, a schedule
detailing all planned experiments. The tasks must correspond to the funds requested.
Describe the methods: what is already available, what must be developed, what assistance is
needed from outside the group. Quality-promoting measures contributing to validity or
plausibility are explicitly welcomed here.

Include: hypotheses, work packages with goals and timelines, methods at a level allowing
feasibility judgement, risk discussion with alternatives, a Gantt chart or equivalent, and —
for multi-applicant proposals — explicit task allocation per applicant.

**2.4 Handling of research data.** Mandatory. Follow the DFG checklist and address:
characteristics and scope of the data; documentation and data quality; storage and technical
archiving; legal obligations and conditions; enabling subsequent reuse and long-term
accessibility; responsibilities and resources. Describe how the participating institutions
contribute to data and information management. If the project does not generate data to a
relevant extent, **state this explicitly**. Costs of collecting research data may be
requested as funding.

**2.5 Relevance of sex, gender and/or diversity.** Describe whether and to what extent sex,
gender and/or diversity are relevant — for persons under study, individuals affected by the
results, animals, samples, the researchers themselves, or otherwise. Relevance varies by
field and by project stage. Address it even if briefly; do not omit.

### 3 Project- and subject-related list of publications

- Only works **cited in sections 1 and 2**.
- No limit on the total number; works not in the public domain may not be cited (exception:
  accepted manuscripts, with the manuscript and the editor's confirmation enclosed).
- **Up to ten of the applicant's own publications may be highlighted** in bold — a global cap
  even where there are several applicants.
- List all authors; do not use "et al.".
- Font ≥ Arial 9 pt.
- Follow form 1.91.

### 4 Supplementary information on the research context (8-page block)

Provide concise but sufficient explanation in proportion to relevance. If a topic is central
to the research question, discuss it under sections 1–2 and cross-reference here.

- **4.1 Ethical and/or legal aspects** — general ethical aspects and anticipated risks or
  harm; for investigations on humans, human material or identifiable data: selection
  criteria for test persons, justification of sample size, potential risks and precautions,
  and the explanation and informed-consent method. Animal experiments require justification
  of the model, estimated numbers per group with statistical design, and implementation of
  the 3Rs. Also covers genetic resources / Nagoya Protocol.
  - **DURC and foreign trade law** — assess whether results could be misused for significant
    harmful purposes; if so, describe the risk/benefit assessment and mitigation, and
    include a KEF statement if institutionally required.
  - **Risks in international cooperation** — where relevant, reflect on risk/benefit and
    mitigation.
  - **Ecological sustainability** — a brief reflection tied specifically to this work
    programme. Research quality remains the priority; additional funds may be requested if
    sustainable approaches cost more.
- **4.2 Employment status** for each applicant (including contract duration and funder).
- **4.3 First-time proposal data**, if applicable.
- **4.4 Composition of the project group** — people working on the project but not paid from
  it. Written explanations of individuals' diversity characteristics are expressly *not*
  wanted.
- **4.5 / 4.6 Cooperation partners** in Germany and abroad. Co-investigators are
  proposal-eligible individuals who neither request nor receive funding. Declarations must
  be attached. For clinical trials, name the responsible biometrician or statistician.
- **4.7 Researchers collaborated with in the past three years** — used to manage reviewer
  conflicts of interest.
- **4.8 / 4.9 Commercial enterprises** — cooperation with, or participation in. See forms
  4.02, 41.028, 41.029.
- **4.10 Scientific equipment** available for the project.
- **4.11 Other submissions** — proposals for this project or major instrumentation submitted
  elsewhere.
- **4.12 Other information.**

### 5 Requested modules/funds

Itemise per applicant and per module, with subtotals and totals, then justify in detail. See
`modules-and-budget.md`.

## 4. Part C — attachments

| Attachment | When |
|-----------|------|
| CV, form 53.200 | **Always**, one per applicant |
| Letters/declarations from cooperation partners | If partners named |
| Ethics vote | Where required; **always** for clinical trials involving humans |
| Employer statement (41.027) | Temporary Position for PIs |
| Declarations 41.028 / 41.029 | Commercial enterprise involvement |
| Interim report | Renewal proposals |
| Manuscript + acceptance confirmation | For accepted-but-unpublished cited work |
| Institutional confirmation of running costs | Instrumentation requests |

**CV rules (53.200):** maximum **four pages**; retain template formatting; Arial ≥11 pt,
spacing ≥1.2; **no photograph**; remove all grey/red instructional text; ORCID requested if
held. Applicants may note circumstances that hampered their scientific work (e.g. childcare,
prolonged illness or disability).

**CV publication list** — two numbered parts:
- **Category A** (mandatory) — articles in peer-reviewed journals, contributions to
  peer-reviewed conferences or anthology volumes, book publications. **Maximum 10.**
- **Category B** (optional) — any other published results: non-peer-reviewed conference
  contributions, preprints, datasets, clinical trial protocols, software, patents, blog
  contributions, infrastructure, transfer, science communication. **Maximum 10.**

These may relate to the whole career and need not be project-related — distinct from
section 3, which is strictly the works cited.

**File rules:** PDF, <10 MB per document, no password protection or restrictions; documents
must be readable, copyable and printable.

## 5. elan submission and naming

1. Register at **elan.dfg.de** — allow ≥48 hours for activation.
2. Ensure all co-applicants are registered.
3. Select the correct programme/call.
4. Complete Part A in the portal.
5. Upload Part B and Part C as separate PDFs.
6. Submit.

For lead-agency procedures (Weave, DACH, DLux, South Tyrol, developing countries, Middle
East), note the supplementary forms 54.013–54.019 and any partner-agency deadline.

**Document naming protocol:**

| Document | Name |
|----------|------|
| Project description (Part B) | `Beschreibung_des_Vorhabens` |
| CV with publication list | `CV_PubList_<last name>` |
| Ethics vote | `Ethikvotum` |
| Employer statement | `Arbeitsplatzzusage` |
| Instrumentation quote | `Angebot_<instrument>_<manufacturer>` |
| Certificates | `Zeugnisse_<last name>` |
| Accepted manuscript | `<year>_<last name>_<keyword>` |
| Acceptance confirmation | `Bestätigung_<year>_<last name>_<keyword>` |

**Final report:** due within three months of the end of the funding period, using form 3.06.
Sections 1–4 are public and may be deposited in a repository; section 5 is non-public.
